1d:T609,The age at which children should start formal schooling is a matter of debate. While some people believe that starting at four gives children an early advantage, others argue that it is better to wait until seven. In my view, a later start is more beneficial, provided that young children have access to good pre-school care.

Those who support an early start argue that young children learn extremely quickly, so beginning lessons at four allows them to develop reading and number skills before their peers. In addition, school provides a structured environment where children from disadvantaged families can receive the support they may not get at home. For working parents, an early start also reduces the cost of childcare.

On the other hand, many educators believe that children under seven learn best through play rather than formal lessons. Activities such as building, drawing and role-play develop language, creativity and social skills, which form the foundation for later academic learning. Finland, where children usually begin school at seven, is often cited as an example: its pupils typically catch up quickly and perform well in international comparisons.
